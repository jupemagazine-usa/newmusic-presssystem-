Quick answer

"Choosin' Texas" by Ella Langley is No. 1 on Billboard's Hot Country Songs for the chart dated October 31, 2026 — its 46th week at the top of that chart, now the second-longest No. 1 run in the chart's history, standing alone. The headline finding this week is a vertical one: the record itself did not move — same four writers, same producers, same named Nashville room, same imprint-first recording line — while every chart around it did: the song returned to No. 1 on the Hot 100 for a record 25th week after three weeks behind "Patient Zero," passed "A Bar Song (Tipsy)" on this chart, and watched its album deliver a fourth Country Airplay No. 1 — and its publishing, mastering, and collection end remains published nowhere, for the third Tuesday review in a row.

What changed — the delta

The count. 44 weeks became 46, through 45 on the chart dated October 24. At 46, the run passes Shaboozey's "A Bar Song (Tipsy)" (45 weeks) and stands alone in second place in the chart's history, behind only Bebe Rexha and Florida Georgia Line's "Meant To Be" (50 weeks, 2017–2018) — four weeks from tying that record, five from breaking it. Sources: https://americansongwriter.com/ella-langleys-choosin-texas-returns-to-no-1-extending-its-record-breaking-run/ · https://www.whiskeyriff.com/2026/10/26/ella-langley-returns-to-1-as-choosin-texas-extends-record-breaking-run-to-25-weeks/

The return on the all-genre chart. After three weeks at No. 2 on the Hot 100 behind Taylor Swift's "Patient Zero," the song returns to No. 1 there for a 25th total week — its seventh distinct stay at the top — on the chart dated October 31. Reported figures for the tracking week October 16–22, 2026: 20 million official U.S. streams (up 1%), 58.6 million radio airplay audience impressions, and 4,000 copies sold, in the song's 53rd week on that chart. "Patient Zero" falls to No. 5. The interim step — chart dated October 24. The song held Hot Country Songs for a 45th week and held No. 2 on the Hot 100, with 19.8 million streams (down 5%, tracking week October 9–15) and a record-tying 26th week at No. 1 on Streaming Songs. That week was not given a separate Tuesday review in this series; it is covered here as the step between 44 and 46. The component crowns pass. On the chart dated October 31 the song falls to No. 4 on Streaming Songs after tying that chart's record with 26 weeks at No. 1, holds its career-high No. 2 on Radio Songs, and moves from No. 2 to No. 5 on Digital Song Sales after a record 19 weeks at No. 1 there. The two genre and all-genre crowns hold; the component crowns are the ones moving. Source: https://www.rockumweb.com/reportes/2667/ella-langley-reclaims-hot-100-no-1-as-choosin-texas-extends-all-time-record-to-25-weeks

The album's radio chain delivers again. "Loving Life Again," from the same album, Dandelion, rises to No. 1 on the Country Airplay chart dated October 31 (32.4 million audience impressions, October 16–22, up 7%, as reported) — the album's fourth Country Airplay No. 1, after this song, "Be Her," and "I Can't Love You Anymore." As reported, Dandelion is just the second album by a woman, or an act with a woman vocalist, to generate four leaders on that chart, the first since Shania Twain's The Woman in Me in 1996, and Langley reaches six career Country Airplay No. 1s in 99 weeks, the fastest for a woman in that chart's history. This is album-level news, entered here as context for the house around this record — it is not a credit on this song. Vertical Integration — Who Owns the Chain

The headline angle of this review, from verified lines only.

Week 46 is a study in a chain that holds still while its numbers move. The song passes one historic run and closes on another, returns to the top of the all-genre chart for a seventh separate stay, and stands as the lead single of an album that just produced its fourth radio No. 1 — and not one line of its own credit chain changed to do any of it. Written by the artist with three partners, co-produced by the artist, played by her working band in a named Nashville room, carried on an imprint-first ℗ line (SAWGOD Records/Columbia) beside a major, promoted to country radio by a named outside specialist (Triple Tigers) — that was the chain at week 43, at week 44, and it is the chain at week 46.

The blank at the end of the chain is also unchanged, and it now has company in plain sight: the album around this song keeps generating documented, chart-certified value in a second format, the writers keep being named in the trade's songwriter chart, and the interpolation of this song's chorus in another house's No. 1 record still carries no published writer credit or clearance line. The work travels. The paper does not. That gap — not the count — is the finding of this review. - Sources: https://americansongwriter.com/ella-langleys-choosin-texas-returns-to-no-1-extending-its-record-breaking-run/ · https://music-world.es/no-1-again-ella-langley-makes-more-history-on-country-airplay-chart/ · https://hallaback.com/the-beatles-drake-hamilton-more-highlights-of-this-week-s-billboard-charts-623656.html
