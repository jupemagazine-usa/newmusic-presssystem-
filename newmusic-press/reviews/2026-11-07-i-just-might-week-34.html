Quick answer

"I Just Might" by Bruno Mars is No. 1 on Billboard's Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs for the chart dated November 7, 2026 — its 34th week at the top, extending the all-time record it has held outright since the chart dated October 24. The headline finding this week is again a vertical one: the count moved, and nothing else did. Not one line of the public credit chain — writers, producers, engineers, video crew, label line — changed to mark a 34th week, and the end of that chain — publishing, studios, collection — is published nowhere, for the third Wednesday review of this song in a row. This week the record did not even get a news story of its own: the week's chart headlines belonged to another chart's record, and this crown advanced on the reference pages alone.

Vertical Integration — Who Owns the Chain

The headline angle of this review, from verified lines only.

Thirty-four weeks is long enough for a chain to be tested twice over. This one lost the top for a week in October, came back, broke a 68-year benchmark, and has now extended it twice — and the public lines never changed. The making still runs through the artist's own long-standing circle: Mars writes and produces with D'Mile, Philip Lawrence, and Brody Brown, arranges the record, and co-directs its video. The recording line still reads "© 2026 Atlantic Records / ℗ 2026 Atlantic Records" — the label's name on both lines. The creative chain is the artist's camp; the recorded chain, as listed, sits on the label's line.

And the blank at the end of the chain is now a record-holder's blank, three weeks past the old record. No publisher, performing-rights home, administrator, or ISRC for this work is public anywhere found — at week 31, at week 33, or at week 34. The longest-running No. 1 in this chart's history collects its writing money through a door no public page names. Q&A

Q: What is the No. 1 song on Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs this week? A: "I Just Might" by Bruno Mars, No. 1 on the chart dated November 7, 2026 — its 34th week at No. 1, extending the chart's all-time record.

Q: How did "I Just Might" get to 34 weeks? A: It tied the record at 31 weeks on the chart dated October 10, lost the top for one week to Drake and Don Toliver's "Solar Eclipse" (chart dated October 17), returned at 32 on the chart dated October 24 — the outright record, passing "Luther" (31) — held at 33 on the chart dated October 31, and holds at 34 on the chart dated November 7. The weeks are cumulative, not consecutive.

Q: Who wrote "I Just Might"? A: Bruno Mars, Dernst Emile II (D'Mile), Philip Lawrence, and Brody Brown — unchanged since release.

Q: What label released "I Just Might"? A: Atlantic Records — both the © and ℗ lines for 2026 read Atlantic Records.

The publishers and the studios are credited nowhere public found, at week 31, week 33, or week 34.
