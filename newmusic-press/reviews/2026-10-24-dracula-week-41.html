Quick answer

"Dracula" (remix) by Tame Impala and Jennie is No. 1 on Billboard's Hot Dance/Electronic Songs for the chart dated October 24, 2026 — a 41st total week on top, up from 39 when this series last reviewed it. The delta in one line: the chain behind the record did not move at all, while everything around it did — a fifth week as the most-heard song on U.S. radio, a return to the song's Hot 100 peak, and one of the record's four public titles quietly retired at the storefront.

  • The count: Hot Dance/Electronic Songs No. 1, 39 → 40 (chart dated Oct. 17) → 41 total weeks (chart dated Oct. 24). Hot Rock & Alternative Songs No. 1, 22 → 23 weeks over the same step. Sources: 00-chart-verification-log.md.
  • The radio crown held and grew: Radio Songs No. 1 for a fifth frame at 63.7 million audience, up 2% (fourth frame, Oct. 17 chart: 62.5 million). Vertical Integration — Who Owns the Chain

The headline angle of this review, from verified lines only.

Two weeks changed every number around this record and no line of the chain itself. The ℗ line still reads "℗ 2026 Columbia Records, a Division of Sony Music Entertainment, under exclusive license from Tame Impala" — the recording is still licensed in from the artist, not owned outright by the major that carries it. Kevin Parker still stands in three rooms at once — writer, sole producer, and at the mix and recording desks — and Jennie is still credited as songwriter and remixer, a working partner in the making, not a voice laid over a finished track. The one new fact about the making cuts the other way from every assumption: the partner's vocal was cut remotely, over Zoom, city to city — the most vertically integrated record on this chart was finished across a video call, and the reporting of that fact is the first time the remix's recording method has been public at all.

The end of the chain is still dark, for a 41st week: no publisher, rights home, administrator, or ISWC for this work is public anywhere found — [needs confirmation] across the whole rights side, unchanged.

Q&A

Q: What is the No. 1 song on Hot Dance/Electronic Songs this week? A: "Dracula" (remix) by Tame Impala and Jennie, No. 1 on the chart dated October 24, 2026, at 41 total weeks at No. 1 for the combined song entry.

Q: What changed for "Dracula" since the last review in this series? A: The count rose from 39 to 41 total weeks at No. 1 on Hot Dance/Electronic Songs; the song took a fifth week at No. 1 on Radio Songs (63.7 million audience, reported up 2%); it returned to its No. 5 Hot 100 peak in a 21st straight week in the top 10; and the Apple Music single page now carries one title where it carried two. The credits, the label line, and the team did not change.

Q: Who wrote the "Dracula" remix? A: Kevin Parker and Sarah Aarons wrote the original; the remix adds Jennie and Carly Gibert as writers — unchanged from the first review.

Q: What label released the "Dracula" remix? A: Columbia Records — under exclusive license from Tame Impala, per the recording line itself. Unchanged.

Q: Who worked on the "Dracula" remix behind the scenes? A: Kevin Parker (production, mix, recording), Eric J Dubowsky, Mendy Stein, Jung Eun-kyung, Loren Humphrey, and mastering engineer Matt Colton, plus the lyric video's credited crew — the roll call is above. The publishers and the remix's studios are credited nowhere public found, for a 41st week.

Q: How was Jennie's part of the remix recorded? A: Remotely, over Zoom, because the artists could not be in the same city — as reported from a Rolling Stone UK interview in October 2026. The studio or studios used are still credited nowhere public.

Q: Where can I watch the official video for the "Dracula" remix? A: The remix has an official lyric video on YouTube; the original recording has a full official video on the tameimpalaVEVO channel. No full music video for the remix has been released in the sources checked.

Q: Where was the "Dracula" remix recorded? A: [needs confirmation] — no studio is named publicly for the remix; the original's studios are documented for the original only. The guest vocal was recorded remotely, per the interview reporting above.
