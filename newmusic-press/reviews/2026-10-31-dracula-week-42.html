Quick answer

"Dracula" (remix) by Tame Impala and Jennie is No. 1 on Billboard's Hot Dance/Electronic Songs for the chart dated October 31, 2026 — a 42nd total week on top, on the chart dated Halloween day itself. The delta in one line: three new songs crowded into the Hot 100's top five above it and pushed the record from its peak to No. 8, and not one of its three No. 1 crowns moved — while the chain behind the record, and every gap in it, stayed exactly where week 41 left them.

Vertical Integration — Who Owns the Chain

The headline angle of this review, from verified lines only.

Vertical headline: the chain did not move — the same artist-held license line, the same nine credited hands, the same dark publishing end — while a crowded Hot 100 pushed the record from its No. 5 peak down to No. 8 without touching any of its three crowns: a 42nd week leading the dance chart, a 24th leading the rock and alternative chart, and a sixth week as the most-heard song on U.S. radio (63.2 million, reported).

The ℗ line still reads "℗ 2026 Columbia Records, a Division of Sony Music Entertainment, under exclusive license from Tame Impala" — the recording is still licensed in from the artist, not owned outright by the major that carries it. Kevin Parker still stands in three rooms at once — writer, sole producer, and at the mix and recording desks — and Jennie is still credited as songwriter and remixer, a working partner in the making. Radio is the lane doing the visible work this week: the audience that keeps this record at No. 1 on Radio Songs for a sixth frame is the same force the week's reporting credits with holding its Hot 100 top-10 place while new releases opened above it.

The end of the chain is still dark, for a 42nd week: no publisher, rights home, administrator, or ISWC for this work is public anywhere found — [needs confirmation] across the whole rights side, unchanged.

Q&A

Q: What is the No. 1 song on Hot Dance/Electronic Songs this week? A: "Dracula" (remix) by Tame Impala and Jennie, No. 1 on the chart dated October 31, 2026, at 42 total weeks at No. 1 for the combined song entry.

Q: What changed for "Dracula" since the last review in this series? A: The count rose from 41 to 42 total weeks at No. 1 on Hot Dance/Electronic Songs; the song took a sixth week at No. 1 on Radio Songs (63.2 million audience, reported) and a 24th week atop Hot Rock & Alternative Songs; three new songs opened in the Hot 100's top five above it, moving it from No. 5 to No. 8 in its 22nd week inside the top 10 in total. The credits, the label line, and the team did not change.

Q: Is "Dracula"'s Hot 100 top-10 run consecutive? A: No — not on the chart history published this week. The song has spent 22 weeks inside the top 10 in total, with time outside the top 10 in between: it first shared the top 10 on the chart dated May 16, 2026, left, and returned to No. 10 in June. Earlier reporting that called the run consecutive is corrected on this record.

Q: Who wrote the "Dracula" remix? A: Kevin Parker and Sarah Aarons wrote the original; the remix adds Jennie and Carly Gibert as writers — unchanged from the first review.

Q: What label released the "Dracula" remix? A: Columbia Records — under exclusive license from Tame Impala, per the recording line itself. Unchanged.

Q: Who worked on the "Dracula" remix behind the scenes? A: Kevin Parker (production, mix, recording), Eric J Dubowsky, Mendy Stein, Jung Eun-kyung, Loren Humphrey, and mastering engineer Matt Colton, plus the lyric video's credited crew — the roll call is above. The publishers and the remix's studios are credited nowhere public found, for a 42nd week.
