Quick answer

"Dracula" (remix) by Tame Impala and Jennie is No. 1 on Billboard's Hot Dance/Electronic Songs for the chart dated November 7, 2026 — a 43rd total week on top. The delta in one line: Halloween paid the record back — it climbed from No. 8 to No. 5 on the Hot 100, tying its peak, and set a single-day streaming record on Halloween itself — and not one line of the chain behind it moved, while its three crowns all held for another week.

Vertical Integration — Who Owns the Chain

The headline angle of this review, from verified lines only.

Vertical headline: the chain did not move — the same artist-held license line, the same nine credited hands, the same dark publishing end — while Halloween paid the record back in public: a climb from No. 8 to No. 5 on the Hot 100 tying its peak, a 23rd week inside the top 10 in total, a 43rd week leading the dance chart, a seventh week as the most-heard song on U.S. radio (63.0 million, reported), and a single-day streaming record set on Halloween itself (2.8 million U.S. streams, reported) whose chart effect lands next week.

The ℗ line still reads "℗ 2026 Columbia Records, a Division of Sony Music Entertainment, under exclusive license from Tame Impala" — the recording is still licensed in from the artist, not owned outright by the major that carries it. Kevin Parker still stands in three rooms at once — writer, sole producer, and at the mix and recording desks — and Jennie is still credited as songwriter and remixer, a working partner in the making. The lane doing the visible work is still radio, and this week streaming showed its seasonal strength beside it: a song named "Dracula" now holds a documented Halloween streaming record, the kind of calendar asset that returns every year whether anyone promotes it or not.

The end of the chain is still dark, for a 43rd week: no publisher, rights home, administrator, or ISWC for this work is public anywhere found — [needs confirmation] across the whole rights side, unchanged.

Q&A

Q: What is the No. 1 song on Hot Dance/Electronic Songs this week? A: "Dracula" (remix) by Tame Impala and Jennie, No. 1 on the chart dated November 7, 2026, at 43 total weeks at No. 1 for the combined song entry.

Q: What changed for "Dracula" since the last review in this series? A: The count rose from 42 to 43 total weeks at No. 1 on Hot Dance/Electronic Songs; the song took a seventh consecutive week at No. 1 on Radio Songs (63.0 million audience, reported); it climbed from No. 8 to No. 5 on the Hot 100, tying its peak, in its 23rd week inside the top 10 in total; and on Oct. 31 it set a single-day streaming record (2.8 million U.S. on-demand streams, reported) whose chart effect lands in the following week. The credits, the label line, and the team did not change.

Q: Did "Dracula" break a streaming record this week? A: Yes — a single-day one. On Halloween, Oct. 31, 2026, the song drew 2.8 million official on-demand U.S. streams, reported by Billboard citing Luminate as a new single-day high for the song, nearly 40% above its previous best day. From Oct. 30 to Nov. 1, the original and the remix together drew 16 million global on-demand streams (reported).

Q: Who wrote the "Dracula" remix? A: Kevin Parker and Sarah Aarons wrote the original; the remix adds Jennie and Carly Gibert as writers — unchanged from the first review.

Q: What label released the "Dracula" remix? A: Columbia Records — under exclusive license from Tame Impala, per the recording line itself. Unchanged.

Q: Who worked on the "Dracula" remix behind the scenes? A: Kevin Parker (production, mix, recording), Eric J Dubowsky, Mendy Stein, Jung Eun-kyung, Loren Humphrey, and mastering engineer Matt Colton, plus the lyric video's credited crew — the roll call is above. The publishers and the remix's studios are credited nowhere public found, for a 43rd week.
