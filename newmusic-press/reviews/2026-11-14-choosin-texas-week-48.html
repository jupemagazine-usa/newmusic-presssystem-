Quick answer

"Choosin' Texas" by Ella Langley is No. 1 on Billboard's Hot Country Songs for the chart dated November 14, 2026 — its 48th week at the top of that chart, two weeks from tying the longest run in the chart's history. The headline finding this week is again a vertical one: the record itself did not move — same four writers, same producers, same named Nashville room, same imprint-first recording line — while the season threw its biggest test at it: Halloween lifted "Thriller" to No. 2 and "Dracula" to No. 3 on the Hot 100 and both fell short; the digital sales crown passed to "Thriller"; the country radio crown came back to this song's own album; the song's radio peak rose to a new high — and its publishing, mastering, and collection end remains published nowhere, for the fifth Tuesday review in a row.

What changed — the delta

The count. 47 weeks became 48. At 48, the run stands alone in second place in the chart's history, behind only Bebe Rexha and Florida Georgia Line's "Meant To Be" (50 weeks, 2017–2018) — two more weeks tie that record, a third breaks it. Sources: https://www.whiskeyriff.com/2026/11/09/ella-langley-continues-her-domination-as-choosin-texas-spends-its-27th-week-at-1-on-the-hot-100/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Billboard_number-one_country_songs_of_2026

The Halloween test, held. The chart dated November 14 is the Halloween frame — tracking October 30 to November 5 — and the season's two biggest records came at the top and stopped one step short. Michael Jackson's "Thriller" re-entered at No. 2 on the Hot 100, a new peak for the song, and Tame Impala and JENNIE's "Dracula" climbed to a new No. 3 high; "Choosin' Texas" held both off for a 27th week at No. 1 on the Hot 100. Reported figures for the chart week: 18.8 million official U.S. streams (down 2%, as reported), 53 million radio airplay audience impressions (down 8%), and 4,000 copies sold (up 5%).
