Quick answer

"This Time" by Kirk Franklin featuring GloRilla is No. 1 on Billboard's Hot Gospel Songs for the chart dated November 7, 2026 — a fifth week on top, stated on the chart page's own No. 1 card. The delta in one line: the double album this single leads was released on October 30 and the crown held straight through album week — while YouTube's credit block for the record now reads two writers and an engineer richer than the credit block this series has carried since the debut, and the owner's name is still missing from that same platform's ℗ line, for a fifth week.

Vertical Integration — Who Owns the Chain

The headline angle of this review, from verified lines only.

Five weeks on top and one album later, no line of this chain has moved. The recording line on the released album's Apple listing still reads Fo Yo Soul Recordings and TRIBL Music Group, under exclusive license to Uptown Records — the artist's own house and its gospel partner at the top, the major division working under their license — and the double album now rides that same line in the open: the single was the lead record of an album cycle held by the same houses in the same order, and the crown held through the album's own release week.

Against that stability, two breaks stand in the paper. On YouTube's auto-generated listing the ℗ line still begins at TRIBL — Fo Yo Soul Recordings, the owner's name, is still missing, for a fifth week. And the credit blocks now disagree with each other: YouTube's block names two writers and an engineer the Shazam/Apple block carried in this series does not, while the immersive engineer in that block appears in YouTube's nowhere — the record's own credits read two different ways on two platforms, in its fifth week at No. 1. The dark end is still dark: no publisher, administrator, or ISWC is public anywhere found — [needs confirmation] across the whole rights side, unchanged.
