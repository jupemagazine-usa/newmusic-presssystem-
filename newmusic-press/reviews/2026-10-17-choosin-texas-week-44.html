Quick answer

"Choosin' Texas" by Ella Langley is No. 1 on Billboard's Hot Country Songs for the chart dated October 17, 2026 — its 44th week at the top of that chart, the third-longest No. 1 run in the chart's history. The headline finding this week is a vertical one: the song's chorus now works inside another house's record — Drake and Don Toliver's "Solar Eclipse," which interpolates it, debuted at No. 5 on the Hot 100 and No. 1 on Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs in this same chart week — while no public source checked names the writers' credit or clearance on that new record, and this song's own publishing side remains published nowhere.

What changed — the delta

The count. 43 weeks became 44. At 44, the run stands third in the chart's history, behind "Meant To Be" (50 weeks) and "A Bar Song (Tipsy)" (45 weeks) — one week from tying the second-longest run. The year mark. The single was released October 17, 2025. On October 17, 2026 — one year to the day, and the chart date of this review — Langley marked the anniversary with an Instagram reel, tagging the three people who wrote the song with her: Joybeth Taylor, Miranda Lambert, and Luke Dick. Her caption, as reported: she is "constantly & consistently amazed by what this song has done," signing off "Here's to 365 days of madness." The chorus in another house. "Solar Eclipse" by Drake with Don Toliver, released October 2, 2026, interpolates "Choosin' Texas" — Billboard's own chart highlights say so in so many words. In this chart week it debuted at No. 5 on the Hot 100 (Drake's record-extending 91st top 10) and at No. 1 on Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs, while Langley's original held at No. 2 on the Hot 100. A country song written in 45 minutes at a retreat is now the foundation of a No. 1 record on another genre's chart. Vertical Integration — Who Owns the Chain

The headline angle of this review, from verified lines only.

Week 44 adds a link no previous week had: this song now sits inside another artist's record. "Solar Eclipse" — a Drake record with Don Toliver, on another genre's chart, at No. 1 there — is built on an interpolation of "Choosin' Texas." The writing room's work has crossed houses. That is vertical reach of a kind money usually buys: the song is earning in a second genre's economy while it still leads its own chart.

But the paper trail does not cross with it. No source checked names a writer credit for the four writers on "Solar Eclipse," names who cleared the interpolation, or names this song's publishers at all — the same blank that stood at week 43. The most-traveled country chorus of the year is working in two houses, and the collection end of its chain is published in neither. - Sources: https://hallaback.com/the-beatles-drake-hamilton-more-highlights-of-this-week-s-billboard-charts-623656.html · https://www.whiskeyriff.com/2026/10/13/drake-scores-top-5-hit-with-solar-eclipse-the-song-that-interpolates-ella-langleys-choosin-texas/
