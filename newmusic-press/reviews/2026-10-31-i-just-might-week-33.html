Quick answer

"I Just Might" by Bruno Mars is No. 1 on Billboard's Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs for the chart dated October 31, 2026 — its 33rd week at the top, extending the all-time record it took outright one week earlier. The headline finding this week is a vertical one: in three weeks the record was interrupted, came back, broke a 68-year benchmark, and extended it — and not one line of its public credit chain moved to do any of it. The chain that was published at week 31 is the chain at week 33, and the end of that chain — publishing, studios, collection — is published nowhere, for the second Wednesday review of this song in a row.

What changed — the delta

The count. 31 weeks became 33, by way of a loss and a return. On the chart dated October 17, Drake and Don Toliver's "Solar Eclipse" took the top for one week (reviewed in this series' Wednesday slot that week). On the chart dated October 24, "I Just Might" returned for its 32nd week — the outright record, passing Kendrick Lamar and SZA's "Luther" (31 weeks across 2024–25). On the chart dated October 31, it holds for a 33rd. The weeks are cumulative, not consecutive: across the year only two songs have displaced it, both featuring Drake — "Janice STFU" (seven weeks, May–June) and "Solar Eclipse" (one week). Vertical Integration — Who Owns the Chain

The headline angle of this review, from verified lines only.

Thirty-three weeks is long enough for a chain to be tested. This one was: it lost the top for a week to a thirteen-writer interpolation record built in another house, came back, and set the record — and the public lines never changed. The making still runs through the artist's own long-standing circle: Mars writes and produces with D'Mile, Philip Lawrence, and Brody Brown, arranges the record, and co-directs its video. The recording line still reads "© 2026 Atlantic Records / ℗ 2026 Atlantic Records" — the label's name on both lines. The creative chain is the artist's camp; the recorded chain, as listed, sits on the label's line.

And the blank at the end of the chain is now a record-holder's blank. No publisher, performing-rights home, administrator, or ISRC for this work is public anywhere found — at week 31 or at week 33. The longest-running No. 1 in this chart's history collects its writing money through a door no public page names.
